# Slide 3: The commons

01 · The commons

## Industries have long drawn on shared resources

### Fisheries
Open waters feed whole fleets. No single company owns the fish, and overfishing depletes what everyone depends on.

### Open source software
Companies build products on code they did not write and do not own, and much of the benefit flows to those who never paid for it.

### The common thread
Value is taken from something held in common. The ethical question is what flows back to it.

# Slide 4: Negative externalities

02 · Negative externalities

## Externalities are costs that someone else pays

### The factory
Produces goods and keeps the profit.

→

### The smoke
Spills into the air as a byproduct of production.

→

### The neighbors
Breathe it, and carry the health costs.

The producer enjoys the benefit. The public absorbs the cost.



# Slide 5: The pattern repeats

03 · The pattern repeats

## AI follows both patterns

### It consumes the commons
Writing, art, music, photography, code, and video that human creators made, gathered at enormous scale to train models.

### It creates externalities
Heat and energy, water for cooling, and eroding trust in what we see and hear. Others carry these costs.



# Slide 6: The costs of AI

03 · The pattern repeats

## The costs of AI that others carry

### Heat and energy
Data centers draw large amounts of power and shed heat into the communities around them.

### Water
Cooling often depends on local water supplies that residents and farms also rely on.

### Trust
Convincing synthetic video and audio make real footage easier to doubt and fakes easier to believe.

# Slide 7: Our approach

03 · The pattern repeats

## Our approach: give back, and bear the load

### Give back to creators
- Prefer providers that license or compensate the work they learn from
- Credit the human work we build on
- Support the open source projects we depend on

### Beneficiaries bear the costs
- Use AI where the value justifies the energy and water it takes
- Choose efficient, right-sized models for the task
- Protect trust by verifying what we ship and being open about AI use

# Slide 9: Air France 447

04 · Human expertise

## Air France 447: when the autopilot handed back control

On June 1, 2009, an Airbus A330 flying from Rio de Janeiro to Paris crossed the Atlantic. Airspeed sensors iced over, the autopilot disengaged, and the crew struggled to fly the aircraft by hand. It crashed into the ocean.

**The autopilot was not the problem. Pilots must keep the skill to step in when it lets go.**

Notes: Please verify the details of the AF447 account against the official BEA report before presenting. The point is not that automation is bad, but that people must retain the skill to take over.

# Slide 11: Keeping our skills

04 · Human expertise

## Keeping the skills that let us judge

### Practice the craft
Do core work yourself on a regular basis so the skill does not fade.

### Review as an expert
Judge AI output against what you know is correct, not against how confident it sounds.

### Know when to step in
Decide up front which parts of a system need a human who can take over.

# Slide 14: When a client does not allow AI

06 · On the job

## When a client does not allow AI tools

### Respect the boundary
Never put client data or code into tools the client has not approved.

### Build skills elsewhere
Practice on personal projects, public data, and sample problems with no client information, within company policy.

### Make the case
Collect concrete examples of the value so we can propose approved tooling to the client.
